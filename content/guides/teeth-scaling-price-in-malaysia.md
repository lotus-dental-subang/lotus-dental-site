## What is Teeth Scaling?

![Teeth Scaling Price in Malaysia](/img/blog/teeth-scaling-1.jpg)

The most typical non-surgical method of treating gum disease, commonly known as periodontitis, is teeth scaling. This will help your gums heal by removing tartar and plaque from your teeth. However, your dentist and periodontist may advise a scale and root planing before the operation, as well as a comprehensive teeth-cleaning before the treatment, especially if you have severe periodontal disease and your condition may require gum surgery.

[Gum disease](https://www.webmd.com/oral-health/gingivitis-periodontal-disease) is mostly brought on by the buildup of a sticky, bacterial-filled plaque along and just below the gum line. Your chance of developing gum disease may rise if your gums are somewhat separated from your teeth, and your dentist may advise teeth scaling. Teeth scaling is a non-surgical operation, however, it is unlike a routine dental cleaning because it entails cleaning the tooth’s sections below the gum line.

## How Do Teeth Scaling Work?

Teeth scaling removes all tartar from your teeth, including the roots, down below the gum line. It is recommended to receive teeth scaling treatment every 6 months.

The teeth scaling procedure is carried out by your dentist using an ultrasonic scaler, a vibrating tool. These actions could happen during teeth scaling:

-   The scaler’s vibrating metal tip removes tartar from your teeth above the gum line.
-   Tartar and plaque from the gum pockets are washed away by spraying water on the scaler.
-   Your dentist utilises a manual (not powered) dental scaler and a scraper to get rid of any last few little bits of tartar.

## When Are Teeth Scaling Necessary?

If your mouth shows evidence of chronic periodontal disease, your dentist will recommend teeth scaling as well as root planing. These procedures can help to alleviate the symptoms of this disorder and keep your mouth healthy.

When bacteria in plaque cause your gums to move away from your teeth, that is when you’ll know you have chronic periodontal disease. This causes big pockets to form between your teeth and gums, where additional bacteria can grow that you cannot reach with at-home oral hygiene.

That is why it is critical to floss regularly to reach areas that toothbrushes cannot.

Chronic periodontal disease, if left untreated, can lead to:

-   Bone and tissue loss
-   Tooth movement
-   Loose teeth
-   Tooth loss

Chronic periodontal disease can cause deep pockets between your gums and teeth, but it can also cause additional symptoms such as:

-   Gums bleeding
-   Gums that are inflamed, red, or sensitive
-   Permanent teeth shifting
-   A change in your bite
-   Bad breath

Teeth scaling and root planing can lessen the pockets that form between your teeth and gums, which lowers your risk of experiencing tooth, bone, and tissue loss brought on by the chronic periodontal disease.
